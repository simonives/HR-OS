# Executive Thesis
This research collection provides a critical academic overview of the evolving landscape of Personnel and HRM, with a strong focus on the psychological and sociological underpinnings of work environments. The authors argue that modern HRM must bridge the gap between empirical research and organisational practice, ensuring that policies are not only efficient but scientifically validated to support long-term sustainability and employee wellbeing.

## Key Architectures & Frameworks
*   **The Scientist-Practitioner Model**: A framework that emphasises applying scientific methods to HR decision-making.
*   **Human Capital Theory**: Theoretical models on how individual knowledge and skills contribute to organistaional value.
*   **Social Exchange Theory**: A lens through which to view the reciprocal relationship between employers and employees.

## Strategic Imperatives
*   Incorporate rigorous, data-driven research into the design of HR interventions (People Analytics).
*   Prioritise the psychological contract between the worker and the firm to drive long-term commitment.
